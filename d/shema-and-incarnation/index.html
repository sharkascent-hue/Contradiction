Does the Shema ('the LORD is one') rule out the Trinity and a God who becomes man?

Deuteronomy 6:4, 'Hear, O Israel: the LORD is our God, the LORD is one (echad)', is Judaism's central creed. Singer argues that Christians who read echad as a 'compound unity' cite only verses where 'one' covers several things, such as one cluster of grapes (Numbers 13:23) or one day (Genesis 1:5).

The vocabulary argument is weak on both sides. Singer is right that echad does not mean 'compound unity', and Christians are right that it does not mean 'absolutely simple' either. The Jewish case is stronger when it leaves vocabulary aside. The Hebrew Bible repeatedly insists that God has no rival and no likeness (Isaiah 40:25, 44:6), and nothing in it teaches a Trinity or a God who becomes human
