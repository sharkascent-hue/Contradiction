Is it unjust for an innocent man to die for others' sins, given Ezekiel 18:20?

'The soul who sins, he shall die. The son shall not bear the iniquity of the father, neither shall the father bear the iniquity of the son' (Ezekiel 18:20; cf. Deuteronomy 24:16). The Quran says the same many times: 'no bearer of burdens will bear the burden of another' (6:164, 17:15, 35:18, 39:7).

Both sides have a fair point. Ezekiel 18 is about human justice and inherited guilt, so it does not squarely address voluntary substitution, which weakens the Muslim proof-text. But penal substitution is philosophically contested even among Christians, and the Muslim intuition that guilt cannot be transferred is widely shared.
